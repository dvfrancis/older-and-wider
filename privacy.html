Privacy and cookies

Last updated 27 November 2026 Plain words, no small print

This site is a home for the podcast and nothing more. It has no forms, no sign-ups and no log-ins, so we never ask for your name, your email address or anything else about you.

A few other companies help the site work. This page tells you what each one stores on your device, and when.

What gets stored, and when

The services this site uses, what each one stores and when
Service What it is for What it stores When
Acast player Plays the episodes on the home page. Two items in your browser storage: AcastLang, a language setting, and token, a player session. Only after you press Play here. We ask again on every visit.
Instagram Shows our latest posts on the message board. Instagram cookies. Only after you press Show the posts here.
Google Analytics Counts visits, so we know which pages people read. Two cookies: _ga, and one whose name starts with _ga_. They last up to two years. Only if you press Accept. Nothing loads if you press Reject or ignore the question.
Google Fonts Sends the lettering you see on every page. Nothing. Google sees your IP address when your browser fetches the fonts. On every page.
Font Awesome, from cdnjs Sends the small icons, such as the play button. Nothing. Cloudflare, which runs cdnjs, sees your IP address when your browser fetches the icons. On every page.
This site Remembers your answer about analytics, so we do not ask on every page. One item in your browser storage, oaw-consent, with your answer and the date. It never leaves your device. When you press Accept or Reject.

Changing your mind

Press Cookie settings at the bottom of any page and pick again. If you press Reject, we delete the Google Analytics cookies straight away.

You can also clear cookies and site data in your browser settings at any time.

With JavaScript turned off, the question does not appear and nothing on the list above loads, apart from the fonts and the icons.

Who looks after this site

Dominic Francis builds and runs this site for the podcast, and he is responsible for your data here. If you have a question about this page, get in touch through his website.

If you are not happy with the answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
